\begin{abstract}
  We present a machine-checked formalization, in the dependently typed language
  Agda, of the pumping lemma for context-free languages by the
  pushdown-automata route: a sufficiently long word accepted by empty stack
  drives the stack through a repeated configuration or to a great height, and in
  both cases the accepting computation is cut into segments that pump. The
  theorem is stated purely about an \emph{arbitrary} pushdown automaton and its
  empty-stack language; grammars are never mentioned. Two ingredients are
  central: a normalization procedure which ensures that
  every step changes the stack height by one, preserving the whole language, the
  empty word included; and a well-founded recursion on the computation length
  that secures a non-empty pumped factor.
\end{abstract}
\keywords{Pumping lemma \and Pushdown automata \and Context-free
  languages \and Formal verification \and Agda \and Dependent types \and
Well-founded recursion.}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
%%---------------------------------------------------------------------------

The pumping lemma for context-free languages (CFLs) is the standard tool for
proving a language \emph{not} context-free. It says that every CFL $L$ has a
constant $p$ such that any word $w \in L$ with $|w| > p$ factors as
$w = u\,v\,x\,y\,z$ with $|v\,y| \geq 1$ and $u\,v^{k}\,x\,y^{k}\,z \in L$ for
every $k \geq 0$. The textbook proof~\cite{hopcroft06,sipser13}, due to
Bar-Hillel, Perles, and Shamir~\cite{BarHillel61}, fixes a grammar in Chomsky
Normal Form and argues on parse trees: a tall enough tree has a root-to-leaf path
repeating a non-terminal, and the subtree at the repetition is what pumps.

A second, less commonly taught route proves the same statement through
\emph{pushdown automata} (PDAs)~\cite{Amarilli12}. The object it argues about is
the \emph{stack profile} of a computation: the sequence of stack \emph{heights}
recorded at each position along the run, from the single symbol present at the
start down to the empty stack at the end. It discards state, input, and the
contents of the stack, keeping only how tall the stack is over time, so that a
computation becomes a path of integers, one whose consecutive values, in the
normal form we use, differ by exactly one. For a PDA accepting by empty
stack in such a normal form, the stack profile of a long accepting computation
behaves in one of two ways. Either the stack stays low and the
computation repeats a configuration, having visited more of them than exist, and so
gives a single loop to iterate; or it climbs high, so two levels are crossed by matching
``surface'' configurations, giving \emph{two} synchronized loops, one growing and
one shrinking the stack, pumped together. The first case yields a single loop,
which we record as $v$, taking $y = \varepsilon$; the second yields the full
$u\,v\,x\,y\,z$ shape. The argument is geometric and never leaves
the world of stacks and computations.

This paper reports a complete, machine-checked formalization of the PDA route in
Agda~\cite{Wadler22}, shaped by two design decisions. \emph{The statement is
about a bare automaton:} we phrase the lemma for the empty-stack language of an
\emph{arbitrary} PDA over finite alphabets, never mentioning grammars in
hypothesis or conclusion. Since every CFL is the empty-stack language of some
PDA, this is the pumping lemma for CFLs, kept entirely inside automata theory.
\emph{Normalization is automaton-to-automaton and language-exact:} the
combinatorics of the stack profile only work when consecutive heights differ by
one. An arbitrary PDA replaces the popped top symbol by a string $\beta$ of any
length, so a single step changes the height by $|\beta| - 1$: it falls by one,
stays put, or rises by an arbitrary amount. Such a profile offers no structure
to argue about.
We therefore first convert $M$ into an equivalent automaton whose every step
changes the stack height by one, and run the geometric argument there. Because
the theorem must speak about the original $M$, such conversion ensures that
the argument will hold for any context-free language.

Our contributions are the following.
\begin{itemize}
    \item We give a direct PDA-to-PDA normalization turning an arbitrary automaton
    into one whose every step changes the stack height by $\pm 1$, preserving the
    full language, $\varepsilon$ included (Section~\ref{sec:normalization}).
    \item We formalize pushdown automata, their empty-stack acceptance, and that
    $\pm 1$ normal form, together with the geometry of stack profiles: a discrete
    intermediate-value theorem, the last- and first-crossing positions of a stack
    level, and a pigeonhole over \emph{surface configurations} that produces the two
    cases of the argument (Sections~\ref{sec:setup} and~\ref{sec:geometry}).
    \item We give the two pumping reconstructions as total functions: a single-loop
    pump for the low-stack case and a synchronized double pump for the high-stack
    case, each producing, for every $k$, an accepting computation of the pumped word
    (Section~\ref{sec:pipeline}).
\end{itemize}

The rest of the paper is organized as follows.
Section~\ref{sec:setup} introduces Agda, pushdown automata, empty-stack
acceptance, and the $\pm 1$ normal form. Section~\ref{sec:overview} gives an
overview of the formalization, showing how its two halves compose into the final
theorem. Section~\ref{sec:normalization} details the normalization of an
arbitrary automaton. Section~\ref{sec:geometry} develops the geometry of stack
profiles. Section~\ref{sec:pipeline} assembles the two pumping reconstructions,
the well-founded non-emptiness argument, and the end-to-end theorem.
Section~\ref{sec:related} discusses related work and
Section~\ref{sec:conclusion} concludes. Proofs are collected in
Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}.

\subsection{An Overview of the Agda Programming Language}

Agda is a dependently typed functional language in the tradition of
Martin-L\"{o}f type theory~\cite{Lof84}. Through the Curry-Howard
correspondence~\cite{Howard80} its types double as propositions and its programs
as proofs, so to establish a theorem is to construct an inhabitant of the type
that encodes it. There is no separate proof calculus: a proof is an ordinary
total function, and an appeal to the induction hypothesis is just a recursive
call.

A small example fixes the notation. Lists, which carry the words, inputs, and
stacks of the whole development, form an inductive type with an empty list and a
cons, and their concatenation recurses structurally on the left argument:
\begin{spec}
\Kw{data} \D{List} (\V{A} : \D{Set}) : \D{Set} \Kw{where}
   \Con{[]}   : \D{List} \V{A}
   \F{\_::\_} : \V{A} \kto{} \D{List} \V{A} \kto{} \D{List} \V{A}

\F{\_++\_} : \D{List} \V{A} \kto{} \D{List} \V{A} \kto{} \D{List} \V{A}
\Con{[]} \F{++} \V{ys} = \V{ys}
(\V{x} :: \V{xs}) \F{++} \V{ys} = \V{x} :: (\V{xs} \F{++} \V{ys})
\end{spec}
The type $\D{Maybe}\;\V{A}$ is equally pervasive. It has two constructors:
$\Con{nothing}$, which carries no value, and $\Con{just}\;\V{a}$, which carries a
single value of type $\V{A}$.

A propositional equality is itself a type, and to prove one is to write a total
function into it. That the empty list is a right unit for concatenation, for
instance, is established by induction on the left list:
\begin{spec}
\F{++-idr} : \kfa{} \{\V{A} : \D{Set}\} (\V{xs} : \D{List} \V{A}) \kto{} \V{xs} \F{++} \Con{[]} \keq{} \V{xs}
\F{++-idr} \Con{[]} = \Con{refl}
\F{++-idr} (\V{x} :: \V{xs}) = \F{cong} (\V{x} ::\_) (\F{++-idr} \V{xs})
\end{spec}
In the base case \AC{\Con{[]}~\F{++}~\Con{[]}} reduces to \Con{[]} by definition,
so \Con{refl} closes the goal; in the step case the induction hypothesis
\AC{\F{++-idr}~\V{xs}} is transported under the cons by \F{cong}. This pairing of
pattern matching on the shape of the data with recursive calls that stand for the
induction hypothesis is the shape of essentially every definition and proof that
follows.

Not all of our recursions decrease structurally; several shrink a numeric measure
instead. For those we lean on the accessibility predicate \D{Acc} of the standard
library:
\begin{spec}
\Kw{data} \D{Acc} (\V{R} : \V{A} \kto{} \V{A} \kto{} \D{Set})(\V{x} : \V{A}) : \D{Set} \Kw{where}
   \Con{acc} : ((\V{y} : \V{A}) \kto{} \V{R} \V{y} \V{x} \kto{} \D{Acc} \V{R} \V{y}) \kto{} \D{Acc} \V{R} \V{x}
\end{spec}
An element is accessible once everything strictly below it already is, and a
relation is \emph{well-founded} when all of its elements are accessible. A
function written by well-founded recursion matches on a certificate
\AC{\Con{acc}~\V{rs}} and reads off, from \V{rs} applied to a proof that $y$ lies
below $x$, the certificate for $y$ that authorizes the recursive call; as that
certificate is a structural sub-term of \AC{\Con{acc}~\V{rs}}, the termination
checker needs no further convincing. We use this scheme with the library's
\F{<-wellFounded} and take the \emph{length of an accepting computation} as the
measure, the recursion that underlies the non-emptiness argument of
Section~\ref{sec:vy}.

One last feature we rely on concerns performance rather than logic. To decide
whether two types are equal the checker \emph{normalizes} terms, unfolding
definitions along the way; by default every definition is \emph{transparent} and
may be unfolded at each of its uses. When a definition has a large normal form yet
its consumers depend on it only through its \emph{type}, this repeated unfolding is
wasted work. An \Kw{opaque} block seals a definition against it: inside the block
the definition computes as usual, but outside it the checker sees only the type
signature and treats the body as inert, like a postulate.
\begin{spec}
\Kw{opaque}
   \F{lemma} : \V{P}
   \F{lemma} = \F{build} \V{args}         -- large, slow-to-normalise normal form

\F{use} : \V{Q}
\F{use} = \F{consume} \F{lemma}
\end{spec}
Elaborating \F{use} then consults only the type \V{P} of \F{lemma}, never its
body, so it type-checks without normalising the expensive term. An \Kw{unfolding}
declaration can still expose the body at a chosen site, making opacity a local,
reversible discipline rather than a permanent abstraction barrier. This is the
lever that keeps the costliest part of our development in check: the high-stack
case (Section~\ref{sec:high}) hands back its witnesses inside a record, and
sealing the function that assembles it stops every module downstream from
rebuilding that construction just to read one of its fields, a point we return to
in Section~\ref{sec:conclusion}.

We point the reader to~\cite{Wadler22} for a fuller account
of Agda.

\subsection{Pushdown Automata and Acceptance}\label{sec:pda}

We fix three types $Q$, $T$, and $\Gamma$ for states, input symbols, and stack
symbols. A pushdown automaton is a record bundling a transition function, an
initial state, an initial stack symbol, and a set of final states: 
\begin{spec}
\Kw{record} \D{PDA} (\V{Q} \V{T} \V{\kg{}} : \D{Set}) : \D{Set} \Kw{where}
   \Kw{field}
      \F{\kd{}} : \V{Q} \kto{} \D{Maybe} \V{T} \kto{} \V{\kg{}} \kto{} \D{List} (\V{Q} \ktimes{} \D{List} \V{\kg{}})
      \F{q\ks{0}} : \V{Q}
      \F{z\ks{0}} : \V{\kg{}}
      \F{isFinal} : \V{Q} \kto{} \D{Bool}
\end{spec}
The transition function takes a state, an \emph{optional} input symbol
(\AC{\Con{nothing}} marks an $\varepsilon$-move, which reads no input), and the
stack symbol on top; it returns a list of alternatives, each a target state
paired with the string $\beta$ that replaces the popped top symbol. The
nondeterminism is the choice of an alternative from this list. A
\emph{configuration}, denoted by type $\D{Conf}\:\V{Q}\:\V{T}\:\V{\kg{}}$, is a
triple of current state, remaining input, and stack. 
Transitions between configurations are denoted by the following type: 
\begin{spec}
\Kw{data} \D{\_\kpda{}\_\kpRa{}\_} (\V{M} : \D{PDA} \V{Q} \V{T} \V{\kg{}}) : \D{Conf} \V{Q} \V{T} \V{\kg{}} \kto{} \D{Conf} \V{Q} \V{T} \V{\kg{}} \kto{} \D{Set} \Kw{where}
   \Con{read} : (\V{r} , \V{\kb{}}) \kin{} \V{M} \F{.\kd{}} \V{q} (\Con{just} \V{a}) \V{A} \kto{}
          \V{M} \kpda{} (\V{q} , \V{a} :: \V{w} , \V{A} :: \V{\kg{}}) \kpRa{} (\V{r} , \V{w} , \V{\kb{}} ++ \V{\kg{}})
   \Con{eps}  : (\V{r} , \V{\kb{}}) \kin{} \V{M} \F{.\kd{}} \V{q} \Con{nothing}  \V{A} \kto{}
          \V{M} \kpda{} (\V{q} , \V{w} , \V{A} :: \V{\kg{}}) \kpRa{} (\V{r} , \V{w} , \V{\kb{}} ++ \V{\kg{}})
\end{spec}
The \Con{read} step consumes the leading input symbol $a$; the \Con{eps} step
leaves the input untouched. Both require the top of the stack to be some $A$,
which they replace by $\beta$, so the new stack is $\beta \mathbin{+\!+} \gamma$.
The reflexive-transitive closure \D{\_\kpda{}\_\kpRas{}\_} is the usual multi-step
relation, built from an empty computation \Con{\ensuremath{\varepsilon}} and a
cons \AC{\_\Con{\ensuremath{\triangleleft}}\_} of a step onto a computation; we
write \F{compLen} for the number of steps in a computation, defined by structural
recursion. A word $w$ is accepted \emph{by empty stack} when, started from
$\F{q}_0$ with the single symbol $\F{z}_0$ on the stack, the automaton can consume
all of $w$ and empty its stack:\footnote{The operator \kSigma{} below is Agda's
dependent pair. Under the Curry-Howard correspondence it is the \emph{existential
quantifier}: an inhabitant is a witness paired with a proof about that witness.
We write \AC{\kSigma{}[ \V{x} ]~\V{P}} for the common case in which the type of
the witness can be inferred.}
\begin{spec}
\F{AccES} : \D{PDA} \V{Q} \V{T} \V{\kg{}} \kto{} \D{List} \V{T} \kto{} \D{Set}
\F{AccES} \V{M} \V{w}
    = \D{\kSigma{}} \kla{} (\V{q} : \V{Q}) \kto{} \V{M} \kpda{} (\V{M} \F{.q\ks{0}} , \V{w} , \V{M} \F{.z\ks{0}} :: []) \kpRas{} (\V{q} , [] , [])
\end{spec}
The final state is irrelevant: acceptance is the existence of \emph{some} state
$q$ in which the input is exhausted and the stack is empty. We write
\[
L(M) \;=\; \{\,w \mid \F{AccES}\;M\;w\,\}
\]
for the language of $M$, and it is this language that the pumping lemma will
speak about.

\section{An Overview of the Formalization}\label{sec:overview}
%%---------------------------------------------------------------------------

The pumping lemma is ordinarily read as an implication to be proved. Read
constructively it is an object to be built, and it is worth being precise about
which one. From an arbitrary automaton $M$ the development must produce a number
$p$; and then, for every word $w$ accepted by $M$ with $p < |w|$, a split of $w$
into five factors, a proof that the middle two are not both empty, and, for
every $k$, an accepting computation of $u\,v^{k}\,x\,y^{k}\,z$. None of these is
a mere existence claim: each is a value that an Agda function computes from the
accepting computation it is handed. The sections that follow are the stages of
that computation.

Neither half carries the statement alone. The geometric argument needs
consecutive heights to differ by exactly one; the obstacle is not that it would
be hard to prove about an arbitrary $M$, but that it cannot even be
\emph{stated} about one, since the positions it names need not exist.
Normalization, conversely, proves nothing about pumping. The theorem is the
composite, and it needs the language equivalence in \emph{both} directions,
forward for $w$ and backward for each of the infinitely many pumped words, and
it needs that equivalence to hold for $\varepsilon$.

A single invocation of the final theorem runs through four movements,
\[
M \;\xrightarrow{\ \text{normalize}\ }\; M'
  \;\xrightarrow{\ \text{profile},\ \text{pigeonhole}\ }\; \text{loops}
  \;\xrightarrow{\ \text{replay}\ }\; u\,v^{k}x\,y^{k}z
  \;\xrightarrow{\ \text{transport}\ }\; M ,
\]
which the rest of the paper carries out in order. The automaton $M$ is
normalized to an $M'$ over the extended stack alphabet $\kmaybe\,\Gamma$,
accepting the same language (Section~\ref{sec:normalization}). An accepted word
$w$ is transported to $M'$ and the stack profile of the resulting computation is
read off, together with its highest point (Section~\ref{sec:geometry}). The
argument branches on that peak: below a threshold a pigeonhole over full
configurations applies, above it one over \emph{surface} configurations, and
either branch yields loops whose $k$-fold replay rebuilds an accepting
computation of the pumped word (Sections~\ref{sec:loops} to~\ref{sec:high}). A
loop that consumes no input is handled instead by a well-founded recursion on
computation length, which is what secures $|v\,y| \geq 1$
(Section~\ref{sec:vy}). Each pumped word is finally transported back to $M$
(Section~\ref{sec:end}).

Two decisions shape the rest: the normalization targets the weaker of two
candidate normal forms (Section~\ref{sec:normalplus} says why), and the pumping
length is computed from the alphabets of $M'$, not of $M$
(Section~\ref{sec:end}).

\section{Normalizing an Arbitrary Pushdown Automaton}\label{sec:normalization}
%%---------------------------------------------------------------------------

The geometric argument of Sections~\ref{sec:geometry} and~\ref{sec:pipeline} runs
only on automata whose every step moves the stack by one. This section defines
that discipline and then builds, from an arbitrary PDA, an equivalent automaton
that obeys it.

\subsection{The $\pm 1$ Normal Form}\label{sec:normal}

We call a PDA \emph{normal} when every transition either pushes nothing,
shrinking the stack by one, or pushes a single new symbol on top of the restored
old top, growing it by one.\footnote{The operator \kuplus{} below is Agda's
disjoint sum of types. Under the Curry-Howard correspondence it is the logical
connective \emph{or}: an inhabitant is a proof of one of the two disjuncts,
carrying a tag that records which of them was proved. That tag is what lets a
consumer branch on the two cases, as \F{normal-step} does just below.}
\begin{spec}
\F{IsNormalPDA} : \D{PDA} \V{Q} \V{T} \V{\kg{}} \kto{} \D{Set}
\F{IsNormalPDA} \V{M} = \kfa{} (\V{q} : \V{Q}) (\V{mt} : \D{Maybe} \V{T}) (\V{A} : \V{\kg{}}) \{\V{r}\} \{\V{\kb{}}\}
   \kto{} (\V{r} , \V{\kb{}}) \kin{} \V{M} \F{.\kd{}} \V{q} \V{mt} \V{A} \kto{} \V{\kb{}} \keq{} [] \kuplus{} \D{\kSigma{}} \kla{} \V{X} \kto{} \V{\kb{}} \keq{} \V{X} :: \V{A} :: []
\end{spec}
The clause $\beta \equiv X :: A :: []$ says a push restores the popped symbol $A$
and stacks a single $X$ above it, so nothing below the top is ever disturbed in a
single step. From this it is immediate that each step is a \emph{pop} or a
\emph{push} on the stack, abstracting away state and input:
\begin{spec}
\Kw{data} \D{StackStep} : \D{List} \V{\kg{}} \kto{} \D{List} \V{\kg{}} \kto{} \D{Set} \Kw{where}
   \Con{pop} : \D{StackStep} (\V{A} :: \V{\kg{}}) \V{\kg{}}
   \Con{push} : \D{StackStep} (\V{A} :: \V{\kg{}}) (\V{X} :: \V{A} :: \V{\kg{}})
\end{spec}
The lemma \F{normal-step} turns any step of a normal automaton into a
\D{StackStep} by branching on which disjunct of \F{IsNormalPDA} the transition
satisfies. The height change is then read off the \D{StackStep} alone:
\begin{spec}
\F{stackStep-\ensuremath{\pm}1} : \D{StackStep} \V{s} \V{s'}
   \kto{} (\F{length} \V{s} \keq{} \Con{suc} (\F{length} \V{s'})) \kuplus{} (\F{length} \V{s'} \keq{} \Con{suc} (\F{length} \V{s}))
\F{stackStep-\ensuremath{\pm}1} \Con{pop}  = \Con{inj\ks{1}} \Con{refl}
\F{stackStep-\ensuremath{\pm}1} \Con{push} = \Con{inj\ks{2}} \Con{refl}
\end{spec}
Both cases are \Con{refl}, and that is the point: the indices of \Con{pop} and
\Con{push} are literally $A :: \gamma$ against $\gamma$, and $A :: \gamma$
against $X :: A :: \gamma$, so each length equation holds by computation, with no
arithmetic to discharge. The $\pm 1$ discipline has no content beyond the shape
of those two indices, and it is exactly what turns a computation into a path of
integers whose consecutive values differ by one (Section~\ref{sec:geometry}).
The other structural fact we take from \Con{push}, used pervasively in
Sections~\ref{sec:geometry} and~\ref{sec:pipeline}, is that it leaves the entire
tail of the stack untouched, so a segment of computation that never descends to a
given depth keeps everything at and below that depth as an invariant suffix.

What the construction must deliver is now fixed by the use made of it: an $M'$
satisfying \F{IsNormalPDA}, over a state type that is still finite (the
pigeonhole of Section~\ref{sec:geometry} counts configurations of $M'$), and a
language equivalence holding for every word, the empty one included. Finiteness
is taken in the constructive sense of a listing of the elements together with a
proof that it is exhaustive, written \F{Finite} in the signatures below.
Theorem~\ref{thm:normalize} below states that contract, and a first reading needs
nothing else from this section.

\subsection{A Stronger Form, and Why We Cannot Target It}\label{sec:normalplus}

There is also a stronger normal form, in which the polarity of a step is fixed by
whether it reads input:
\begin{spec}
\Kw{record} \D{IsNormalPDA\kp{+}} (\V{M} : \D{PDA} \V{Q} \V{T} \V{\kg{}}) : \D{Set} \Kw{where}
   \Kw{field}
      \F{read-pops}  : (\V{r} , \V{\kb{}}) \kin{} \V{M} \F{.\kd{}} \V{q} (\Con{just} \V{a}) \V{A} \kto{} \V{\kb{}} \keq{} []
      \F{eps-pushes} : (\V{r} , \V{\kb{}}) \kin{} \V{M} \F{.\kd{}} \V{q} \Con{nothing} \V{A} \kto{}
                  \D{\kSigma{}} \kla{} \V{X} \kto{} \V{\kb{}} \keq{} \V{X} :: \V{A} :: []
\end{spec}
Every \D{IsNormalPDA\kp{+}} automaton is normal, i.e. $\D{IsNormalPDA\kp{+}}\to \D{IsNormalPDA}$. 
The strengthening matters for the empty word. In an \D{IsNormalPDA\kp{+}}
automaton, every read pops and every $\varepsilon$-move pushes, so emptying a
stack that starts at height one requires the number of reads to exceed the number
of $\varepsilon$-moves by exactly one; since the reads consume the input, an
accepted word has length at least one. \emph{No \D{IsNormalPDA\kp{+}} automaton
accepts $\varepsilon$ by empty stack.} Consequently a normalization that is to
preserve the full language of an automaton accepting $\varepsilon$ \emph{cannot}
target \D{IsNormalPDA\kp{+}}; it must target the weaker \D{IsNormalPDA}, where a
height-decreasing step may be an $\varepsilon$-move and $\varepsilon$ may be
accepted. This is why the construction below empties the stack by an
$\varepsilon$-move, and it is the reason the non-emptiness of the pumped factor
will need a separate argument (Section~\ref{sec:vy}).

The stronger form is not thereby discarded, and the development keeps both
theorems from a single geometric core. The high-case reconstruction
(Section~\ref{sec:high}) is carried out once, under the weaker hypothesis, and
the proof that $|v\,y| \geq 1$ travels with its result as a \emph{function
awaiting} an \D{IsNormalPDA\kp{+}} witness: it is discharged by the counting
argument just given, but only once such a witness is supplied. A caller holding
one applies it and gets the bound for free; the arbitrary-automaton path leaves
it unapplied, because its normalized automaton has none, and pays for the bound
with the well-founded recursion instead.

\subsection{The Construction}\label{sec:construction}

Two obstacles separate an arbitrary $M$ from the $\pm 1$ discipline: a transition
may push a string $\beta$ of any length, not just zero or one symbol, and the
automaton may inspect or empty its initial stack symbol $\F{z}_0$ in ways the
discipline forbids. We remove both by extending the stack alphabet and adding a
small set of bookkeeping states.

Write $\bot$ for \Con{nothing} and $\widehat{A}$ for $\Con{just}\,A$. Since the
construction slices one transition of $\delta$ into several unit steps, the
bookkeeping states have to be indexed by the transition being sliced: we let
$\tau$ range over the transitions of $M$, where $\tau$ is given by a state $q$,
an optional input symbol $\mathit{mt}$, a top symbol $A$ and a choice of
$(r,\beta) \in \delta\,q\,\mathit{mt}\,A$, and we write $\beta_\tau$ for the
string that $\tau$ pushes. The normalized automaton $M'$ is then
\[
\Gamma' = \kmaybe\,\Gamma,
\qquad
\F{z}_0' = \bot,
\qquad
Q' = \{\iota\}
   \cup \{\langle q\rangle\}_{q \in Q}
   \cup \{\langle \tau\rangle\}_{\tau}
   \cup \{\langle \tau,j\rangle\}_{1 \leq j < |\beta_\tau|},
\]
with a stack $A_1 \cdots A_n$ of $M$ encoded as
$\widehat{A_1} \cdots \widehat{A_n}\,\bot$. Its transitions, for every
$(r,\beta) \in \delta\,q\,\mathit{mt}\,A$ with $\beta = B_1 \cdots B_k$, writing
$\tau$ for that transition, are
\[
\begin{array}{r@{\;\ni\;}l@{\qquad}l}
\delta'(\iota, \varepsilon, \bot)
  & (\langle \F{q}_0\rangle,\; \widehat{\F{z}_0}\,\bot) & \\[2pt]
\delta'(\langle q\rangle, \mathit{mt}, \widehat{A})
  & (n_0,\; \varepsilon)
  & n_0 = \langle r\rangle \text{ if } k = 0, \text{ else } \langle \tau\rangle \\[2pt]
\delta'(\langle \tau\rangle, \varepsilon, C)
  & (n_k,\; \widehat{B_k}\,C)
  & n_k = \langle r\rangle \text{ if } k = 1, \text{ else } \langle \tau,k-1\rangle \\[2pt]
\delta'(\langle \tau,j\rangle, \varepsilon, C)
  & (n_j,\; \widehat{B_j}\,C)
  & n_j = \langle r\rangle \text{ if } j = 1, \text{ else } \langle \tau,j-1\rangle \\[2pt]
\delta'(\langle q\rangle, \varepsilon, \bot)
  & (\langle q\rangle,\; \varepsilon) &
\end{array}
\]

The first rule installs the encoded initial stack. The second turns $\tau$ into a
pop of the encoded top symbol, reading an input symbol exactly when
$\mathit{mt}$ is one, and branches on $k$: an empty push string ends the macro at
once, otherwise control passes to the push chain. The third and fourth rules run
that chain, stacking the symbols of $\beta$ in reverse so that $B_1$ ends on top,
each of them restoring the symbol beneath and so a legal $\pm 1$ push. A macro
therefore replaces $\widehat{A}$ by $\widehat{B_1} \cdots \widehat{B_k}$, exactly
what $\tau$ does, while every individual step is a $\pm 1$ pop or push. The last
rule pops the marker, emptying the stack; this is the step that lets $M'$ accept
the empty word, and the one \D{IsNormalPDA\kp{+}} would forbid
(Section~\ref{sec:normalplus}). The resulting automaton is normal by
construction.

Two details of $Q'$ are worth pausing on. The families $\langle \tau\rangle$ and
$\langle \tau,j\rangle$ are kept apart because $n_0$ is chosen by branching on
$k$, a plain natural number, at a point where $k$ may still be zero and no
position into $\beta$ can be formed. And $Q'$ is finite as soon as $Q$, $\Gamma$
\emph{and} $T$ are, the last because $\tau$ records the optional input symbol;
finiteness of $T$ is a mild and standard assumption, and it is what keeps the
pigeonhole of Section~\ref{sec:geometry} available on the normalized automaton.

That $M'$ is normal is immediate from the shape of the five rules. What the rest
of the paper needs from it is the following.

\begin{theorem}[\F{normalize}]\label{thm:normalize}
Let $M$ be a pushdown automaton over finite $Q$, $T$ and $\Gamma$. The automaton
$M'$ above is normal, its state type is finite, and $L(M') = L(M)$.
\end{theorem}

\section{The Geometry of Stack Profiles}\label{sec:geometry}
%%---------------------------------------------------------------------------

Fix a normal automaton $M$ (the $M'$ just constructed, or any other; nothing
below depends on where it came from) and an accepting computation
$d : M \kpda{} (\F{q}_0 , w , \F{z}_0 :: []) \kpRas{} (q , [] , [])$ of length
$m = \F{compLen}\;d$. Indexing positions $0,\dots,m$ along $d$, we write
$\F{configAt}\;k\;d$ for the configuration reached after $k$ steps,
$\F{stackAt}\;k\;d$ for its stack, and
$
\F{s}_{\mathsf h}\;k\;d \;\triangleq\; \F{length}\,(\F{stackAt}\;k\;d)
$
for the \emph{stack height} at position $k$; these are projections of an
auxiliary \F{splitAt} that cuts $d$ at position $k$ into a prefix and a suffix
and is the workhorse for all segment surgery. The height profile starts at
$\F{s}_{\mathsf h}\,0\,d = 1$ (the lone symbol $\F{z}_0$) and ends at
$\F{s}_{\mathsf h}\,m\,d = 0$ (the empty stack), and by \F{stackStep-\ensuremath{\pm}1}
consecutive heights differ by one. We also write $\F{consumed}\;d$ for the input
read along $d$; for an accepting computation $\F{consumed}\;d = w$, which
underlies the factorization of $w$ into the segments below.

\subsection{A Discrete Intermediate-Value Theorem}\label{sec:ivt}

Because the height profile moves by single steps, it cannot skip a value: every
height between the heights of two endpoints is realized at some intermediate
position. This is a discrete intermediate-value theorem, proved by induction on
the number of steps with a case analysis on the sign of each step:

\begin{lemma}[\F{ivt}]\label{lem:ivt}
Let $a + \mathit{steps} \leq m$. For every height $h$ lying between
$\F{s}_{\mathsf h}\,a\,d$ and $\F{s}_{\mathsf h}\,(a+\mathit{steps})\,d$
(in either order), there is a position $p$ with $a \leq p \leq a+\mathit{steps}$
and $\F{s}_{\mathsf h}\,p\,d \equiv h$.
\end{lemma}

The name is more than analogy, though the hypotheses do not transpose verbatim.
Over $\mathbb{N}$ every function is continuous, so reading Lemma~\ref{lem:ivt}
as an instance of the classical statement would leave it with no hypothesis at
all. What does the work is a Lipschitz condition: a function is Lipschitz with
constant $K$ when $|f(x) - f(y)| \leq K\,|x-y|$ for all $x,y$, which is strictly
stronger than continuity ($\sqrt{x}$ on $[0,1]$ is continuous but not
Lipschitz~\cite{Bartle11}) and, on a domain whose smallest increment is $1$, says
exactly that consecutive values lie within $K$ of each other. Here it holds with
$K = 1$, since \F{stackStep-\ensuremath{\pm}1} makes consecutive heights differ
by exactly one; the normalization of Section~\ref{sec:normalization} is therefore
not a convenience here, it is the hypothesis of the lemma. Completeness of
$\mathbb{R}$, in turn, is replaced by finiteness of the interval, so the proof is
an induction on $\mathit{steps}$ rather than a passage to a supremum, and it
computes the witness $p$.

From \F{ivt} we extract, for a chosen height $h$ at or below a reference position
$j$, two distinguished positions: the \emph{last} position up to $j$ at which the
profile sits at height $h$, and the \emph{first} position from $j$ onward at which
it does. Each comes with an extremality certificate (no later, respectively
earlier, position in the relevant range has height $h$):
\begin{itemize}
\item $\F{lp}\;h$ : the last-pass position $p \leq j$ with
$\F{s}_{\mathsf h}\,p\,d \equiv h$ and no $q \in (p,j]$ of height $h$;
\item $\F{fp}\;h$ : the first-pass position $p \geq j$ with
$\F{s}_{\mathsf h}\,p\,d \equiv h$ and no $q \in [j,p)$ of height $h$.
\end{itemize}
Their existence is supplied by \F{ivt} applied to the ascending part of the
profile up to the peak and to the descending part after it, and the extremality
is obtained by walking outward from the witnessed positions.

\subsection{Equal Heights Force Equal Stacks}\label{sec:stackeq}

The extremality of \F{lp} and \F{fp} feeds the single most useful geometric
lemma: between two positions of \emph{equal} height that is a floor for
everything in between, the stacks are not merely of equal length but
\emph{identical}.

\begin{lemma}[\F{stackAt-eq}]\label{lem:stackeq}
If $a \leq b \leq m$, the heights at $a$ and $b$ are equal, and no position in
$[a,b]$ has smaller height, then $\F{stackAt}\;a\;d \equiv \F{stackAt}\;b\;d$.
\end{lemma}

\subsection{Surface Configurations and the Two Cases}\label{sec:twocases}

Let $B = \F{s}_{\mathsf h}\,j\,d$ be the peak height, where $j$ is the position of
a global maximum of the profile, computed by a routine \F{argmax}. Set
\[
P \;\triangleq\; |Q|^{2}\cdot|\Gamma| \;=\; |Q \times \Gamma \times Q|,
\qquad
\F{pumpLen} \;\triangleq\; |Q|\cdot(|\Gamma|+1)^{\,P+1},
\]
the latter being the number of pairs of a state and a stack of height at most
$P+1$ (recorded as a vector of $P+1$ optional stack symbols, with
$\F{card\mbox{-}Cfg}$ computing it exactly). More generally we write
$|\mathrm{Cfg}_{\leq h}|$ for the number of such pairs with stack height at most
$h$, so that $\F{pumpLen} = |\mathrm{Cfg}_{\leq P+1}|$. Finiteness of $Q$ and
$\Gamma$ makes both numbers computable. The pumping length will be \F{pumpLen}; the dichotomy is
whether the peak exceeds $P+1$.

\paragraph{Low case ($B \leq P+1$).}
The whole profile is bounded by $B \leq P+1$, so every configuration along $d$
has a stack of height at most $P+1$ and is therefore one of at most \F{pumpLen}
distinct pairs (state, bounded stack). When $|w| > \F{pumpLen}$, the computation
has more than \F{pumpLen} steps, hence visits more than \F{pumpLen} positions and,
by a pigeonhole over these finitely many configurations, repeats one: there are
positions with the same state and the very same stack. The computation between
them is a loop returning to an identical configuration.

\paragraph{High case ($B > P+1$).}
Here we look not at full configurations but at \emph{surface} ones. To each
stack level $g$ we associate the triple
\[
\big(\;\F{state at}\;\F{lp}(g),\;\;\F{top symbol at}\;\F{lp}(g),\;\;
\F{state at}\;\F{fp}(g)\;\big)\;\in\;Q \times \Gamma \times Q,
\]
recording the state and exposed top symbol when the profile last leaves level $g$
on the way up, and the state when it first returns to level $g$ on the way down.
There are exactly $P = |Q|^{2}\cdot|\Gamma|$ such triples. Since the peak exceeds
$P+1$, there are more than $P$ levels strictly between the base and the peak, so
two of them, $g < h$, carry the same surface triple
(\F{fullstate-collision}, a pigeonhole over $Q \times \Gamma \times Q$). This
matching of surface configurations is what makes the two loops, one between the
last passes of $g$ and $h$ on the way up and one between their first passes on
the way down, splice together coherently.

%%---------------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{From Stack Profiles to the Pumping Lemma}\label{sec:pipeline}
%%---------------------------------------------------------------------------

Both halves are now in place: an automaton whose every step moves the stack by
one, and a geometry that locates loops in its profile. This section turns those
loops into the lemma.

\subsection{Iterating a Loop}\label{sec:loops}

Both cases reconstruct pumped computations from \emph{loops}, segments that return
to a matching configuration, and two pieces of surgery from our trace library make
this possible. \F{append-stack} replays a computation on top of arbitrary extra
stack, by the invariant-suffix property of Section~\ref{sec:normal};
\F{swap-input} replays it after an arbitrary passive input suffix, since a
computation reads only the prefix it consumes. Together they let a loop be
\emph{repeated}: a segment that reads $v$ and grows the stack from
$X :: \gamma$ to $X :: (m'' \mathbin{+\!+} X :: \gamma)$ can be run $n$ times to
read $v^{n}$ and stack $n$ copies of the block $m''$
(\F{pump-up}); symmetrically \F{pump-down} runs a stack-shrinking loop $n$
times to read $y^{n}$.

\subsection{The Low Case: a Single Loop}\label{sec:low}

In the low case the pigeonhole yields a loop whose endpoints share state
\emph{and} stack. \F{find-loop} extracts it as a factorization of $d$ into a
prefix $d_1$, a loop $d_2$ returning to the same configuration, and a suffix
$d_3$ that empties the stack, together with the corresponding split
$w = u \mathbin{+\!+} v \mathbin{+\!+} x$ of the input, where $v$ is the input
read by $d_2$. Because the endpoints' stacks are literally equal, no
\F{append-stack} adjustment is needed: the loop can be excised or repeated in
place. \F{pump-from-loop} then turns this into, for every $n$, an accepting
computation of $u \mathbin{+\!+} v^{n} \mathbin{+\!+} x$; the case function
\F{caseLow-N} produces the five-part factorization with $y = z = \varepsilon$, the tail $x$
above serving as its middle component:
\[
\begin{array}{l}
\F{caseLow-N} \;:\; (d : \text{an accepting computation of } w) \to (p' : \knat)\\
\quad \to (\text{every stack along } d \text{ has height at most } p')
   \;\to\; |\mathrm{Cfg}_{\leq p'}| < |w| \\
\quad \to \bigl(\exists\,u,v,x.\;\; w = u\,v\,x \;\wedge\; |v| > 0
   \;\wedge\; \forall n.\; u\,v^{n}x \text{ is accepted}\bigr)\\
\quad \uplus\;\; \bigl(\exists\, d'.\;\; d' \text{ accepts } w \text{ and }
   \F{compLen}\;d' < \F{compLen}\;d\bigr)
\end{array}
\]
The length hypothesis is stated at the height bound $p'$ actually supplied, not
at \F{pumpLen}: the function needs only that $w$ outruns the configurations
available below $p'$. The caller passes the peak $B$ for $p'$ and discharges the
hypothesis from $\F{pumpLen} < |w|$ by monotonicity of $|\mathrm{Cfg}_{\leq h}|$
in $h$ (\F{card-Cfg-mono}), which is where $B \leq P+1$ is used. The left
disjunct is
the factorization; the right disjunct is the shorter computation produced when
the loop $d_2$ reads no input, discussed in Section~\ref{sec:vy} below.

\subsection{The High Case: a Synchronized Double Pump}\label{sec:high}

The high case is the substantial one. From the colliding levels $g < h$ we read
off three segments of $d$, delimited by the four positions $\F{lp}(g)$, $\F{lp}(h)$,
$\F{fp}(h)$, $\F{fp}(g)$ in this order: the ascending loop from $\F{lp}(g)$ to
$\F{lp}(h)$ that reads $v$ and raises the stack; the middle segment from
$\F{lp}(h)$ to $\F{fp}(h)$ that reads $x$; and the descending loop from
$\F{fp}(h)$ to $\F{fp}(g)$ that reads $y$. The prefix before $\F{lp}(g)$ reads $u$,
the suffix after $\F{fp}(g)$ reads $z$, and altogether
$w = u \mathbin{+\!+} v \mathbin{+\!+} x \mathbin{+\!+} y \mathbin{+\!+} z$. Two
applications of Lemma~\ref{lem:stackeq} give the stack equalities
$\F{stackAt}\,\F{lp}(g) \equiv \F{stackAt}\,\F{fp}(g)$ and
$\F{stackAt}\,\F{lp}(h) \equiv \F{stackAt}\,\F{fp}(h)$, which are what make the two
loops compatible.

The reconstruction is performed by \F{double-pump}, which, given the prefix, the
ascending loop, the middle, the descending loop, the suffix, and an iteration
count $n$, glues them into an accepting computation of
$u \mathbin{+\!+} v^{n} \mathbin{+\!+} x \mathbin{+\!+} y^{n} \mathbin{+\!+} z$.
The synchronization is the delicate part: running the ascending loop $n$ times
stacks $n$ copies of a block $X :: m''$, and the descending loop must remove
exactly those copies, in order, after the middle segment has run on top of them.
The key reshaping is that the stack after reading $v^{n}$ begins with $X$, so the
middle and descending phases can be run with the right invariant suffix beneath
them; this is captured by a small equation $\F{rep}\;n\;(X :: m'') \mathbin{+\!+}
X :: \delta \equiv X :: \gamma$, where $\F{rep}\;n\;t$ is $t$ repeated $n$ times,
the $t^{n}$ of the statements above, and discharged by \F{append-stack} and
\F{swap-input}. The bridge that turns a raw segment of $d$ into a loop of exactly
the polymorphic shape \F{double-pump} expects is \F{segment-to-loop}, which strips
the invariant floor from the segment and reattaches it generically.

\F{caseHigh-N} has the same sum return type as \F{caseLow-N}, its left disjunct
built from \F{double-pump}.

\subsection{A Non-Empty Pumped Factor, and the Lemma for Normal
Automata}\label{sec:vy}

The pumping lemma requires $|v\,y| \geq 1$: the pumped factor must consume at
least one input symbol, or iterating it would not change the word and the
statement would be vacuous. In \D{IsNormalPDA\kp{+}} this is automatic: reads pop
and $\varepsilon$-moves push, so a loop, whose endpoints have equal stack height,
performs as many reads as $\varepsilon$-moves, and a non-empty loop therefore
reads at least one symbol. This counting argument (\F{balance}) is how the
textbook presentations obtain $|v\,y| \geq 1$, and how our
\D{IsNormalPDA\kp{+}} variant \F{pda-pumping} does too.

It fails for the weaker normal form \D{IsNormalPDA}, the one the construction of
Section~\ref{sec:normalization} is obliged to target if the empty word is to be
preserved: a loop there may consist entirely of
$\varepsilon$-moves, pushing and popping without reading anything, and would
consume nothing. We replace the counting argument by a \emph{minimality}
argument. Observe that each case function already accounts for this possibility:
its return type is a sum, and when the discovered loop consumes no input it
returns, instead of a factorization, a strictly shorter accepting computation of
the \emph{same} word. In the low case this shorter computation is the loop-free
$d_1$ followed by $d_3$ (the empty loop $d_2$ is excised); in the high case it is
$\F{double-pump}\dots 0$, the $k=0$ reconstruction, which skips the two loop
segments and so is strictly shorter, the loops having length at least one because
they cross stack levels. That the reconstructed computation is strictly shorter is
discharged from \F{compLen}-preservation lemmas for the surgery operations
(\F{compLen-swap}, \F{compLen-append}, \F{compLen-strip}, and a substitution
lemma proved by the $J$ rule).

This sets up a recursion. A long word either pumps outright, or it has a
strictly shorter accepting computation, on which we try again. Since computation
length is a natural number, the recursion terminates, and at the bottom the loop
necessarily consumes input.

The recursion is packaged as a generic engine (\F{pump-go}), parameterized by the
statement $P$ to be established and by a single \emph{step} carrying out one
round of the dichotomy. Applied to an accepting computation $d'$ of $w$, a step
returns either $P$ itself or a second accepting computation $d''$ of the same
$w$ with $\F{compLen}\;d'' < \F{compLen}\;d'$. The engine consumes the first
outcome by returning it and the second by recursing on $d''$, and it is the
\D{Acc} scheme of Section~\ref{sec:setup} that licenses the recursion: the
recursive call is guarded by the accessibility certificate for
$\F{compLen}\;d''$, read off the certificate for $\F{compLen}\;d'$ that the
engine matched on. The entry point \F{pump-run} starts it from
\F{<-wellFounded}.

Instantiating the engine with the step that runs \F{argmax}, takes the dichotomy,
and dispatches to \F{caseLow-N} or \F{caseHigh-N} gives the pumping lemma for an
arbitrary \emph{normal} automaton:

\begin{theorem}[\F{pda-pumping-N}]\label{thm:normal}
Let $M$ be a normal pushdown automaton over finite $Q$ and $\Gamma$. For every
$w$ with $\F{AccES}\;M\;w$ and $\F{pumpLen} < |w|$, there are $u,v,x,y,z$ with
$w = u\,v\,x\,y\,z$, $|v\,y| \geq 1$, and
$\F{AccES}\;M\;(u\,v^{k}\,x\,y^{k}\,z)$ for every $k$.
\end{theorem}

The \D{IsNormalPDA\kp{+}} theorem \F{pda-pumping} is the same statement with the
counting argument in place of the recursion, and is retained as the special case
obtained through \F{normal\kp{+}\ensuremath{\Rightarrow}normal}; building
\F{pda-pumping-N} did not disturb it.

\subsection{The End-to-End Theorem}\label{sec:end}

Composing the normalization of Section~\ref{sec:normalization} with
Theorem~\ref{thm:normal} gives the pumping lemma
for an arbitrary pushdown automaton. The constant it produces is \F{pumpLen}
computed from the alphabets of the \emph{normalized} automaton, not of $M$.
Because the normalization is language-exact, the empty word needs no separate
handling: $\varepsilon$ falls under the
vacuous case $p < |\varepsilon| = 0$ of the quantifier, and every pumped word
is transported back and forth through the equivalence unconditionally.

\begin{theorem}[\F{pumping-pda}]\label{thm:end}
For every pushdown automaton $M$ over finite $Q$, $T$, and $\Gamma$, there is a
$p \in \knat$ such that for every $w \in L(M)$ with $p < |w|$ there
are $u,v,x,y,z$ with $w = u\,v\,x\,y\,z$, $|v\,y| \geq 1$, and
$u\,v^{k}\,x\,y^{k}\,z \in L(M)$ for every $k$.
\end{theorem}

\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}
%%---------------------------------------------------------------------------

The proof we formalize is due to Amarilli and Jeanmougin~\cite{Amarilli12}, who
gave the pushdown-automata argument for the context-free pumping lemma as an
alternative to the classical parse-tree proof of Bar-Hillel, Perles, and
Shamir~\cite{BarHillel61}. Their account is informal; we make every step of the
stack-profile geometry, the two-case pigeonhole, and the loop reconstructions
explicit and machine-checked, and we add the well-founded minimality argument and
the language-exact normalization that the informal version does not need to spell
out.

The theorem itself has been mechanized before. Ramos et
al.~\cite{RamosPumping16} formalize the context-free pumping lemma in Rocq by the
classical route, deriving it from a Chomsky Normal Form grammar and a long
derivation tree, in a development of more than twenty thousand lines; they later
put it to work, proving specific languages non-context-free and context-free
languages not closed under intersection~\cite{RamosApplications19}. Barthwal and
Norrish~\cite{Barthwal10,Barthwal10thesis} reach the same theorem in HOL4, within
a substantial fragment of context-free language theory, in a classical setting
where the witnessing functions are not extracted as programs. Three things set
the present development apart. It argues on automata and never builds a tree, so
the combinatorics is the geometry of a stack profile rather than the branching of
a derivation. Its statement quantifies over an arbitrary pushdown automaton and
mentions no grammar, in hypothesis or conclusion. And it is some 2,300 lines,
which the shift of route, rather than any economy of style, is what buys.

Mechanizations of the surrounding theory are more common. Firsov and
Uustalu~\cite{Firsov15} certify the conversion of grammars to Chomsky Normal Form
in Agda and~\cite{Firsov14} CYK parsing for CNF grammars, working with parse
trees. Ramos et al.~\cite{Ramos15} formalize grammar simplification, and
Dvo\v{r}\'{a}k and Blanchette~\cite{Dvorak23} verify closure properties of
general grammars in Lean, using grammar-based constructions in place of the usual
Turing-machine arguments. The recent unified Isabelle/HOL formalization of
context-free grammar theory by Nipkow et al.~\cite{NipkowLRY-IJCAR26} covers
normal forms and several deep theorems on the grammar side. The pumping lemma
through pushdown automata, with a language-exact PDA normal form as we use here,
is to our knowledge not among the existing machine-checked treatments.

Pumping has also been studied formally for its own sake, and on the regular side
constructively. Doczkal, Kaiser and Smolka~\cite{Doczkal13} develop a constructive
theory of regular languages in Rocq, the pumping lemma among its results, in some
1,400 lines of Ssreflect, later extended to a systematic account of regular
language representations~\cite{DoczkalSmolka18}. Hobor, Li and
Stephan~\cite{Hobor19} formalize the pumping properties of Jaffe and of
Ehrenfeucht, Parikh and Rozenberg, and find that the classical proof that block
cancellation implies regularity appeals to the axiom of choice; they give a proof
that does not. Their concern is ours one level up: a constructive reading of a
pumping statement has to produce its witnesses, which is why our factorization is
computed rather than asserted and why the non-emptiness of the pumped factor
costs a well-founded recursion (Section~\ref{sec:vy}) instead of a counting
argument. Certified parsing of regular expressions~\cite{Lopes16,Cardoso23} is a
complementary line sharing our broader goal of a verified language-theory
library.

%% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
%%---------------------------------------------------------------------------

We have presented a complete, machine-checked Agda formalization of the pumping
lemma for context-free languages by the pushdown-automaton route, stated directly
about the empty-stack language of an arbitrary automaton. Its core is a $\pm 1$
normal form, reached by a language-exact normalization, whose stack profile
admits a discrete intermediate-value theorem and a pigeonhole over surface
configurations, and a well-founded recursion securing a non-empty pumped factor
where the textbook counting argument does not apply.

The development is sixteen modules and some $2{,}300$ non-blank, non-comment
lines on top of a small library of general-purpose utilities, with no postulates,
termination pragmas, or unfinished goals. Its cost is concentrated in the high case, which an \Kw{opaque}
interface around the assembled witnesses keeps from being re-elaborated
downstream.

Nothing in the stack-profile layer mentions pumping, and it is the part most
likely to outlive the theorem proved here. In future work we intend to reuse it,
as it stands, for three results. The first is \emph{Ogden's lemma}, which demands
that the pumped factors carry a marked position: the pigeonhole of
Section~\ref{sec:twocases} would range only over crossings that still have marked
input ahead of them, forcing the loop it returns to consume a mark, while the
geometry itself stays untouched. The second is \emph{pumping downwards}, since
the shortening step of Section~\ref{sec:vy} already bounds the shortest accepted
word, which is what emptiness and finiteness procedures stated on the automaton
would rest on. The third is the \emph{grammar-level statement} of the pumping
lemma, reached through the standard grammar-to-PDA construction.

All source code, and instructions on how to build it, are available
online~\cite{repo}.

\section{Proofs}\label{app:proofs}
%%---------------------------------------------------------------------------

For the reader's convenience each statement is repeated before its proof. Except
in the first, the notation is that of Section~\ref{sec:geometry}: $M$ is a normal
automaton, $d$ an accepting computation of length $m$, and
$\F{s}_{\mathsf h}\,k\,d$ the stack height at position $k$.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Theorem~\ref{thm:normalize} (\F{normalize}).}
\emph{Let $M$ be a pushdown automaton over finite $Q$, $T$ and $\Gamma$. The
automaton $M'$ of Section~\ref{sec:construction} is normal, its state type is
finite, and $L(M') = L(M)$.}
\begin{proof}
Normality is read off the five rules: each is a pop, replacing the top symbol by
the empty string, or a push of one symbol above the restored top. Finiteness of
$Q'$ follows from that of $Q$, $\Gamma$ and $T$, since a transition $\tau$ is
determined by a state, an optional input symbol, a top symbol and an index into
the list $\delta$ returns for them.

The equivalence is a step-by-step bisimulation organized around the stack
invariant: whenever $M$ is in state $q$ with stack $s$, the simulation is in
$\langle q\rangle$ with $s$ encoded above the marker. Completeness
(\F{normalize-complete}) is by induction on a computation of $M$, replacing each
transition by its macro and the start and end by the marker push and pop; an
$\varepsilon$-computation of $M$ becomes an $\varepsilon$-computation of $M'$, so
the empty word is preserved. Soundness (\F{normalize-sound}) inverts each macro
by four mutually recursive functions that follow $M'$ structurally, using the
non-emptiness of the intermediate stacks to rule out spurious base cases; a stack
invariant on the push-slicing states $\langle\tau\rangle$ and
$\langle\tau,j\rangle$ tracks how far a macro has progressed. \qed
\end{proof}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Lemma~\ref{lem:stackeq} (\F{stackAt-eq}).}
\emph{If $a \leq b \leq m$, the heights at $a$ and $b$ are equal, and no position
in $[a,b]$ has smaller height, then
$\F{stackAt}\;a\;d \equiv \F{stackAt}\;b\;d$.}
\begin{proof}
The segment from $a$ to $b$ never descends below the common height, so the stack
present at $a$ is an invariant suffix throughout: by the push-preserves-tail
property of normal steps it is carried unchanged to $b$ as a suffix of
$\F{stackAt}\;b\;d$. Since the two stacks have the same length, the part above
the suffix is empty, and the stacks coincide. \qed
\end{proof}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Theorem~\ref{thm:normal} (\F{pda-pumping-N}).}
\emph{Let $M$ be a normal pushdown automaton over finite $Q$ and $\Gamma$. For
every $w \in L(M)$ with $\F{pumpLen} < |w|$, there are $u,v,x,y,z$
with $w = u\,v\,x\,y\,z$, $|v\,y| \geq 1$, and
$u\,v^{k}\,x\,y^{k}\,z \in L(M)$ for every $k$.}
\begin{proof}
By \F{pump-run} on the input computation. Each round computes the peak via
\F{argmax} and branches on $B \leq P+1$. The low branch calls \F{caseLow-N},
whose left disjunct is converted to the five-part shape by taking
$y = z = \varepsilon$ (so $u\,v^{k}\,x\,y^{k}\,z$ collapses to $u\,v^{k}\,x$,
matching the disjunct's word after rewriting $\F{rep}\;k\;[] = []$), and whose
right disjunct is fed back to the recursion; the high branch calls
\F{caseHigh-N} similarly. Termination is the well-founded recursion on
\F{compLen} of Section~\ref{sec:vy}; at every leaf the discovered loop consumes
input, so $|v\,y| \geq 1$. \qed
\end{proof}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Theorem~\ref{thm:end} (\F{pumping-pda}).}
\emph{For every pushdown automaton $M$ over finite $Q$, $T$, and $\Gamma$, there
is a $p \in \knat$ such that for every $w \in L(M)$ with $p < |w|$
there are $u,v,x,y,z$ with $w = u\,v\,x\,y\,z$, $|v\,y| \geq 1$, and
$u\,v^{k}\,x\,y^{k}\,z \in L(M)$ for every $k$.}
\begin{proof}
Normalize $M$ to $M'$, with the language equivalence and the finiteness of its
state and stack alphabets. Take $p = \F{pumpLen}$ computed from those sizes. Given
$w \in L(M)$ with $p < |w|$, transport it to $w \in L(M')$ by
completeness, apply Theorem~\ref{thm:normal} to obtain the factorization and the
per-$k$ memberships $u\,v^{k}\,x\,y^{k}\,z \in L(M')$, and transport each
back to $M$ by soundness. \qed
\end{proof}
